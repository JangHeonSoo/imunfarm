Aquaponics videos make it look simple: put a vegetable bed over a fish tank and pump water between them. Then you start shopping and the questions pile up. How big a tank? How many watts of pump? How much clay media? How many fish?

This guide fills the cart for one **100L fish tank** on a balcony, with what to buy, what it costs in Korea and how to assemble it. Sizing follows the FAO small-scale aquaponics manual.

> Bottom line: **keep fish under 1kg in a 100L tank, build a 0.3–0.5㎡ grow bed 15–20cm deep, and pick a pump that turns the tank over once or twice an hour. Parts start at about 230,000 won on a sunny balcony, or about 340,000 won with a grow light and timer.**

## 1. What 100L can support

The most common DIY mistake is overstocking. FAO puts maximum density at **20kg of fish per 1,000L**, and advises **halving it to 1kg per 100L for tanks under 500L**. FAO also advises against growing food fish in tanks that small, so balcony systems suit **ornamental fish such as goldfish or guppies plus leafy greens and herbs**.

Grow area follows feed. FAO uses **40–50g of feed per ㎡ per day for leafy greens** and 50–80g for fruiting crops.

| Total fish weight | Daily feed (1–2% of body weight) | Leafy green area |
| ----------------- | -------------------------------- | ---------------- |
| 0.5kg             | 5–10g                            | about 0.1–0.25㎡ |
| 1kg               | 10–20g                           | about 0.2–0.5㎡  |

A 100L tank matches **0.3–0.5㎡** of grow bed. Start with about 0.5kg of fish and increase as water stabilizes.

## 3. Grow bed: 1:1 with the tank, depth by crop

Oklahoma State University Extension recommends a **1:1 ratio of media bed volume to fish tank volume** for basic flood-and-drain systems. FAO recommends **15–20cm depth for leafy greens and 30cm for fruiting crops**. A 0.5㎡ bed at 20cm is exactly 100L.

If you use a deep storage box as the bed, fill only 15–20cm of media and give it a flat, solid base. Media adds up: a 0.4㎡ bed at 15–20cm needs **60–80L** of clay.

## 4. Choose the pump by flow, not watts

FAO advises circulating the full tank volume **once per hour at low density, twice at high density**, so 100–200L/h for 100L. FAO's rule of thumb is **about 40L per hour per watt**, so 3–5W could be enough in theory. Flow drops with lift height, so choose a pump with headroom and throttle it with a valve.

## 5. Clay pebbles vs gravel; avoid limestone

| Media           | Surface area   | Pros                  | Cons                       |
| --------------- | -------------- | --------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Expanded clay   | 250–300㎡/㎥   | light, easy to handle | expensive                  |
| Volcanic gravel | about 300㎡/㎥ | nearly inert          | heavy                      |
| Limestone       | 150–200㎡/㎥   | cheap                 | raises pH; not recommended |

Aquaponics runs at pH 6–7, and limestone keeps pushing pH up. On a balcony, light **expanded clay** in 8–20mm size is the safest choice.

## 6. Seven assembly steps

1. **Pick the spot.** A filled 100L tank weighs over 100kg; choose a solid floor near a drain.
2. **Fill the tank** with dechlorinated water.
3. **Mount the bed** above or beside the tank with a drain back to the tank.
4. **Rinse and add media** to 15–20cm.
5. **Connect pump and hose.** A bell siphon floods and drains automatically but needs tuning; continuous flow is simpler for a first build.
6. **Add an air stone** so oxygen continues if the pump stops.
7. **Cycle for four weeks** before adding fish. See the [aquaponics biofilter setup guide](/en/blog/aquaponics-biofilter-ammonia-ph-setup/).

## 7. Water quality targets

| Parameter        | Target      |
| ---------------- | ----------- |
| Temperature      | 18–30℃      |
| pH               | 6–7         |
| Dissolved oxygen | above 5mg/L |
| Ammonia          | below 1mg/L |
| Nitrite          | below 1mg/L |

A Korean study of home guppy aquaponics suggests 21–25℃ and pH 6.0–7.5 with lettuce, and found its system drifting to pH 7.91, too high for lettuce. Check pH regularly.

## FAQ

**How many fish go in a 100L aquaponics tank?**
Count by weight: under 1kg per FAO, starting near 0.5kg.

**How much does DIY aquaponics cost?**
About 230,000 to 340,000 won for the parts in this guide.

**How much clay media do I need?**
Bed area × depth: 60–80L for a 0.4㎡ bed at 15–20cm.

## Conclusion

Balcony aquaponics succeeds on ratios, not big equipment: under 1kg of fish per 100L, a 0.3–0.5㎡ bed 15–20cm deep, and a pump turning the tank over once or twice an hour. Cycle the water for four weeks, then add fish slowly. For other fish options, see [aquaponics fish recommendations](/en/blog/aquaponics-fish-recommendation/).
