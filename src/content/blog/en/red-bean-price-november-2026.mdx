Dongji, the winter solstice day when Koreans eat red bean porridge, falls in late December. Buyers ask whether to wait; growers ask whether to sell now.

> Bottom line: <strong>On November 13, 2026, KAMIS domestic red beans (top grade, 40 kg) averaged 631,000 won across five cities, about 15,775 won per kg.</strong> That is 3.9% lower than October 8 and 14.4% lower than a year earlier. Seoul has held at 724,000 won since September, while Gwangju fell to 460,000 won. Online, the October premium for new-crop beans has disappeared: a 2026-crop 1 kg bag sold for 15,740 won.

Earlier records: [Red bean price outlook, October 2026](/blog/red-bean-price-outlook-2026-harvest/) and [Red bean harvest time 2026](/blog/red-bean-harvest-time-2026/).

The Daegu–Gwangju gap grew from 218,000 to 271,000 won per 40 kg. Imported red beans averaged 259,000 won (about 6,475 won per kg). See [domestic](/prices/red-bean/) and [imported](/prices/red-bean-red/) red bean prices.

## Does the price rise before Dongji?

Seoul monthly averages from November to December over 2018–2025 rose in four years and fell in four (+17.5%, −5.6%, +3.3%, −15.2%, −0.7%, +2.4%, +38.4%, −0.7%). The calendar alone does not predict December.

## Rules of thumb

- Buyers: little reason to wait. Online domestic red beans were 12,250–15,950 won per kg on November 16. Buy what you will use; store sealed in a cool place because of bean weevils.
- Growers: which market you sell to matters more than when. On the same day, Daegu and Gwangju differed by 271,000 won per 40 kg. If you hold until Dongji or Lunar New Year, store below 8°C as the Rural Development Administration advises, or seal with oxygen absorbers and desiccant (weevil emergence fell from 17.7% to 3.7% in a 2017 trial).
