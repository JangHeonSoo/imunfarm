Dried persimmon slices are a classic Korean winter snack, but prices are all over the place. Sweet persimmon prices also fell sharply over the past month. Is it cheaper to buy the slices or dry your own?

> Bottom line: <strong>On November 22, 2026, a 1 kg bag of daebong dried persimmon slices cost 19,950 won on Coupang, and a Cheongdo daebong bag 22,900 won.</strong> On November 20, KAMIS recorded top-grade Fuyu sweet persimmons at 37,200 won per 10 kg in Seoul, down 14.5% on the month. Fresh persimmon is about 84% water, so drying 8 kg of flesh yields only about 2 kg. Ingredient cost alone comes to roughly 17,500 won per kg at wholesale, so home drying costs about the same as buying.

## 3. Yield and electricity

Korea's food composition data lists raw sweet persimmon at 84.1 g water per 100 g. A Korean study of dried persimmon slices found finished moisture of 38–46%. Dried weight = flesh weight × 15.9% ÷ (100% – final moisture): about 245 g, 265 g and 290 g per kg of flesh at 35%, 40% and 45% moisture.

A 250 W dehydrator uses 2.5 kWh per 10 hours, about 540 won at the KEPCO tier-2 energy rate (214.6 won/kWh, before other charges). Three 20-hour batches for 10 kg of fruit come to about 3,200 won, or roughly 1,500 won per kg of finished slices.

## 4. For growers

Drying mid-grade fruit removes cosmetic defects from the equation, but weight falls to about a quarter. Count peeling labor, power, packaging and shipping, and check with the local government food hygiene office whether selling dried fruit requires a business registration.
