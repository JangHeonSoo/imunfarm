Peanut pods ripen underground, which makes it hard to judge when to dig. Dig too early and the kernels are underfilled; too late and pods sprout or break off in the soil. Poor drying invites mold. And once dug, timing the sale changes what you receive.

> Bottom line: <strong>Korea's Rural Development Administration puts the harvest window for seed peanuts at 100–110 days after flowering, late September to early October.</strong> Peanuts for boiling fresh are dug about 80 days after flowering, in early September. Dry pods fully to about 8% moisture and store them in the shell. On 10-year averages, wholesale prices peak in October and drop 6% in November as the new crop arrives. On October 2, 2026, top-grade domestic peanuts sold for KRW 486,000 per 30 kg in Seoul.

## 1. When to dig.

Count days from flowering, not sowing. Before the main harvest, dig a few test plants: well-matured pods have a clear net-like vein pattern and a thick, hard shell; soft shells with faint veins are not ready. Dig when most pods are mature, since waiting for every pod lets the earliest ones spoil.

## 2. Drying and storage.

Plants are often turned over and field-dried before picking pods, then pods are sun-dried on nets rather than bare soil. Dry to about 8% moisture and store in the shell somewhere cool and dry. Bring nets in before rain.

## 3. When to sell.

Ten-year Seoul averages (2016–2025) rise slowly from January (KRW 372,545) to October (KRW 406,833), then fall 6% in November (KRW 382,512). Holding until spring was a coin toss: March beat October in only 4 of 9 years, with swings from −23% to +26%.

## 4. This year.

Seoul has held at KRW 486,000 since September 1, 10% below a year earlier but 19% above the 10-year October average. Daegu paid KRW 540,000 and Daejeon KRW 450,000, a 20% gap. These are wholesale intermediary prices; growers receive less at auction.

## 5. Conclusion.

Judge the harvest by the pods, using 100–110 days after flowering as the guide, dry to 8% moisture, and sell what you can in October before new-crop supply pushes November prices down.
