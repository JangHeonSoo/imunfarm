Ground perilla seed thickens Korean soups such as deulkkae sujebi. Domestic hulled powder sells online for KRW 40,000–75,000 per kilogram, while the raw seed costs about KRW 16,600 per kilogram wholesale.

> Bottom line: <strong>On October 11, 2026, domestic hulled perilla powder on Coupang cost about KRW 40,000–75,000 per kilogram. On October 8, domestic perilla seed (grade A, 23 kg) averaged KRW 381,800 across five wholesale cities, about KRW 16,600 per kilogram</strong>, so powder sells at 2.4–4.5 times the seed price. Geopi and talpi both mean hulled. Keep the powder in the freezer.

## 1. Hulled powder and prices

Geopi (去皮) and talpi (脫皮) are the same thing: seed with the hull removed, then ground. Sellers often use both words in one product name. Plain "perilla powder" may include the hull, and saengdeulkkae powder is made from unroasted seed. We found no official definitions from the RDA or the food safety ministry.

Coupang prices on October 11 (domestic, hulled, before coupons): 500 g at KRW 19,800 (KRW 39,600/kg, 2026 crop), 700 g at KRW 38,020 (about 54,300/kg), 200 g at KRW 12,070 (60,350/kg), 100 g at KRW 7,290 (72,900/kg) and a cooperative 200 g pack at KRW 14,990 (74,950/kg). Smaller packs cost more per kilogram.

## 2. Grinding at a mill

Buying new-crop seed and having a local mill grind it is cheaper on paper. We found no public price list for grinding, so ask first about the fee, washing charges, whether the mill can hull, and the minimum amount.

## 3. Storage

About 60% of perilla seed fat is alpha-linolenic acid. In a National Institute of Crop Science trial, the acid value of stored seed rose 97% in a year at 25°C and 57% at 4°C, and the authors advised cold storage before February for October-harvested seed. The National Institute of Agricultural Sciences says to keep perilla powder in the freezer or a cool, dry place. Freeze it in single-use portions and discard it if it smells rancid.

## 4. For farms selling powder

Article 25(2)(6) of the Enforcement Decree of the Food Sanitation Act exempts simple processing such as cutting, peeling, drying, salting, aging or heating from business reporting, but grinding is not on the list. A Chuncheon health office said in 2023 that grinding dried chili into powder counts as manufacturing. Farms planning to sell ground perilla should check with their city or county hygiene office before setting up. An on-site manufacturing and sales business may sell only directly to end consumers.
