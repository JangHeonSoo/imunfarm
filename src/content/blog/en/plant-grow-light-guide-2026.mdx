Balcony lettuce stalls after November. Cold is part of it, but short, low winter sun is the bigger problem. A grow light fixes the light; the questions are how strong, how long and how much power.

> Bottom line: <strong>Korean lettuce LED studies used a 16-hour-on, 8-hour-off cycle at around 200 µmol·m⁻²·s⁻¹.</strong> On a balcony that gets some sun, 12–16 hours on a timer is enough. Under the KEPCO residential tariff (tier 2, including climate and fuel-cost charges and VAT), a 36 W light running 14 hours a day uses 15.1 kWh a month, about 3,800 won.

## 1. Hours per day

- Sunny balcony: 12–14 hours, starting in the morning.
- North-facing or indoor: 16 hours.
- Always give at least 8 hours of darkness; running 24 hours only raises the bill.

## 3. What to check

- Wattage from the spec table, not the product title.
- PPFD stated together with a distance.
- White light is easier on the eyes in living spaces.
- Adjustable height and a built-in or plug-in timer.
