For older adults in the countryside, walking means getting groceries, going to the field and visiting the village hall. When a cane no longer feels steady, families start looking for a walker.

> Bottom line: <strong>A rollator ("silver car") with wheels, brakes and a seat suits longer walks with rests. A lighter walker without brakes suits indoor and short-distance use.</strong> Older adults with a long-term care grade can buy an adult walker as welfare equipment from the National Health Insurance Service (NHIS), paying 15% (9% or 6% if reduced, nothing for Medical Aid type 1) within a yearly limit of 1.6 million won.

## Rollator or walker

| Type     | Features                               | Best for                                  |
| -------- | -------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Rollator | Wheels, brakes and locks, seat, basket | Long walks, shopping, rests along the way |
| Walker   | No brakes or locks, lighter            | Indoors and short distances               |

On cracked village roads and dirt paths, larger wheels roll over bumps more easily but add weight. Fold and lift it before buying if it often goes into a car.

## Safety

- Lock the wheels before adjusting the height.
- Match the height to the user. NHIS warns that handles set too high can increase the risk of a bent posture.
- Do not lean your full weight on it.
- Take care at thresholds and steps, and lock the brakes before sitting.

## Long-term care welfare equipment

| Item              | Rule                                                   |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Yearly limit      | 1.6 million won per person; the excess is paid in full |
| Copay             | 15%, reduced 9% or 6%, exempt for Medical Aid type 1   |
| Adult walker      | Purchase item, service life 5 years                    |
| Cane              | Purchase item, service life 2 years                    |
| Registered models | 63 adult walkers (Notice No. 2026-180)                 |

For a walker with a 150,000 won benefit price, the copay is 22,500 won at 15%, 13,500 won at 9% and 9,000 won at 6%. Take the long-term care certificate and welfare equipment confirmation to a registered welfare equipment provider. Adult walkers are bought, not rented.

Registered people with disabilities may instead use the NHIS assistive device benefit. It pays 90% of the standard amount (100% for near-poverty households), and the device must be bought from a registered seller within six months of the prescription date.
