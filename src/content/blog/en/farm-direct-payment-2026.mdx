Once the rice is cut, the question going around Korean villages is the same every November: has the direct payment come in yet. A neighbouring county has been paid, your account is quiet, and nobody quite remembers the rate.

> Bottom line: <strong>the 2026 basic public-interest direct payment is paid by city and county offices from November through year-end.</strong> In July the agriculture ministry said recipients and amounts would be fixed by the end of October and paid from November to the end of the year. As of the morning of November 12, 2026 the ministry had not yet published a release announcing the start of this year's payment (last year: announced November 19, paid from November 20). Rates are unchanged: KRW 1.3 million per household for small farms, and KRW 1.36–2.15 million per hectare for area payments. If nothing arrives, ask the local eup/myeon/dong office or the direct payment call centre at 1334.

> Earlier record: registration as a farm business entity, the first condition, is covered in [Korea Farm Business Registration 2026](/en/blog/farm-business-registration-2026/).

## 1. What the payment is

What farmers call the "farm direct payment" is officially the basic public-interest direct payment, created in 2020 when the rice, upland and less-favoured-area payments were merged. It has two forms, and a household receives only one: a flat small-farm payment, or an area payment calculated from hectares. Optional payments such as the strategic crop payment are separate schemes.

## 2. When it is paid in 2026

| Date                 | What happened                                                                          | Source                   |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| Mar 1 – May 31, 2026 | Applications (online Mar 1 – May 31, in person Mar 3 – May 29)                         | Ministry release, Feb 26 |
| Jul 14               | 1.332 million applications, 1.046 million ha; recipients to be fixed by end of October | Ministry release         |
| Sep 30               | Deadline for change registration and mandatory training                                | Ministry releases        |
| Nov – Dec            | Payment                                                                                | 2026 schedule            |

There is no single national pay date. The ministry transfers funds to provinces and cities or counties, which check bank account errors and then pay farmers in turn. That is why one county is paid days before the next.

In 2025 the ministry announced the payment on November 19 and began paying on November 20: KRW 2.3843 trillion to 1.285 million farm households and farmers (1.045 million ha). Small-farm payments went to 530,000 households (KRW 686.5 billion) and area payments to 760,000 farmers (KRW 1.6978 trillion). The 2026 total and headcount have not been published yet.

## 3. Small-farm payment: KRW 1.3 million per household

The amount is flat (Enforcement Decree, Article 8) and unchanged in 2026. All conditions must be met.

| Condition                                | Threshold                                                           |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Eligible farmland of the household       | 0.1 ha to 0.5 ha                                                    |
| Farmland owned by household members      | Under 1.55 ha in total                                              |
| Farming                                  | 3 or more consecutive years before applying                         |
| Rural residence                          | 3 or more consecutive years before applying                         |
| Off-farm income                          | Under KRW 20 million per person, under KRW 45 million per household |
| Livestock / protected cultivation income | Under KRW 56 million / under KRW 38 million                         |

Miss one and the household is paid by area instead. This is the usual reason a newly returned farmer receives much less than KRW 1.3 million.

## 4. Area payment: KRW 1.36–2.15 million per hectare

Rates from the 2026 application notice (January 9, 2026), unchanged from 2025. Unit: KRW 10,000 per hectare.

| Farmland                                       | Up to 2 ha | Over 2 to 6 ha | Over 6 ha |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ---------: | -------------: | --------: |
| Agricultural promotion area (paddy and upland) |        215 |            207 |       198 |
| Paddy outside promotion area                   |        187 |            179 |       170 |
| Upland outside promotion area                  |        150 |            143 |       136 |

One hectare of paddy inside a promotion area is the basis for KRW 2.15 million; one hectare of upland outside it, KRW 1.5 million. The payable area is capped at 30 ha for individuals and 50 ha for farming corporations.

## 5. Eligibility: the off-farm income cap rose to KRW 47 million

You must actually farm land registered under your farm business registration, use at least 1,000㎡ (0.1 ha) for farming, and stay under the off-farm income cap. On August 26, 2026 the ministry announced the cap would rise from KRW 37 million to KRW 47 million, effective November 13, 2026, and said it also applies to those who applied for the 2026 area payment. It expects about 10,000 more recipients.

The land itself must also qualify under Article 8 of the Act, so check newly bought land with the local office before applying. First-time applicants need at least one year of farming 0.1 ha or more in the previous three years, or annual sales of KRW 1.2 million or more.

## 6. If the amount was reduced

Recipients must keep 16 compliance rules: four in the Act (maintaining farmland, pesticide and fertiliser standards, training) and twelve in the Enforcement Decree (change registration, farm waste management, farm records and others). Reductions apply per item: 10% the first time, 20% the second, 40% from the third.

- Mandatory training: the 2026 deadline was September 30. Missing it costs 10%.
- Change registration: not updating changed land or crops costs 10%, applied from this year according to an April 2026 release.

## 7. If nothing has arrived

1. Check whether your city or county has started paying. Payment continues through year-end.
2. Check the bank account on your application. Closed or mismatched accounts are caught in the pre-payment check.
3. Check that you were registered. If you received a refusal notice, you can request re-examination within 14 days through the eup or myeon head (Enforcement Rule, Article 15).
4. Call 1334 (extension 4) for the scheme, or 1644-8778 for farm business registration records.

We could not confirm whether the Nongup-ez portal (nongupez.go.kr) has a payment lookup menu, nor an official deadline for disputing the amount paid. Ask the local office.

## 8. Fraud

A landowner who does not farm but collects the payment commits fraud. The Act allows recovery, a surcharge of up to five times the amount (Article 20) and a registration ban of up to eight years (Article 19). Tenant farmers should hold a written lease and register in their own name. From 2026, applicants with a long-term care grade must submit a medical certificate showing they can farm (ministry explanatory note, October 23, 2026).

## 9. The farmer allowance is a different payment

The "farmer allowance" (nongmin sudang) is paid by provinces and counties under local ordinances, not by the national scheme. Two confirmed 2026 examples: South Chungcheong pays KRW 800,000 a year to single-person households and KRW 450,000 per person in larger households, from August 3; North Gyeongsang pays KRW 600,000 per farm household in April to June. It is applied for and paid separately.

## 10. Before next year's application

The 2027 schedule has not been announced. Register any land bought or leased over winter, note that land accepted so far on a "not unlawfully occupied" confirmation is excluded from 2027 under the 2026 notice, recheck eligibility under the higher income cap, and keep farm records.
