A pack of oyster mushrooms usually lasts two or three meals. By the second meal, moisture has gathered in the fridge and the cap edges are going soft.

> Bottom line: <strong>Keep oyster mushrooms in the fridge only for a short time. For longer storage, rinse lightly, blanch briefly, tear them, squeeze out the water and freeze them in zipper bags (Foodnuri, Korea's public food information site).</strong> Do not eat wild "oyster mushrooms" picked in the mountains, because poisonous look-alikes exist. On November 23, 2026, KAMIS top-grade oyster mushrooms in Seoul sold for 19,500 won per 2 kg, 31.8% higher than a year earlier.

Earlier record: [Mushroom prices, October 2026](/blog/mushroom-price-october-2026/).

## In the fridge

- Do not wash before storing. Wash just before cooking.
- Wrap the cluster in a paper towel and put it in a zipper bag or a sealed container.
- Keep the cluster whole. Torn pieces go soft from the cut side.

We found no official number of days for a home fridge. In a 2011 study by the Gyeonggi-do Agricultural Research and Extension Services, oyster mushrooms wrapped in film and kept at 4±1°C stayed marketable for 24 days when grown at 13°C and 18 days when grown at 16°C. Those were freshly harvested mushrooms under lab conditions, so a home fridge will be shorter.

## In the freezer

1. Rinse lightly.
2. Blanch briefly in boiling water.
3. Tear into pieces and squeeze out the water.
4. Freeze in zipper bags, flat and in single portions.
5. Thaw naturally before cooking.

The "10 days chilled, 20 days blanched, 6 months frozen" figures in an RDA mushroom guide refer to maitake, not oyster mushrooms.

## Wild oyster mushrooms

KAMIS only tracks cultivated oyster mushrooms. The Korea Forest Service says oyster-type mushrooms appear most from late summer to early autumn, the same season as poisonous look-alikes:

| Poisonous mushroom              | Why it looks similar       | Clue                                                       |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hwagyeong-beoseot (Omphalotus)  | Grows on wood like oysters | A large black spot inside the stem when split              |
| Satgat-oedae-beoseot (Entoloma) | Called "ground oyster"     | Grows from soil, common from late August to late September |

The Rural Development Administration (RDA) says the safest choice is not to eat any wild mushroom. Of 2,292 native mushroom species, only 416 (18%) are edible and 248 are poisonous. Symptoms usually appear within 6–12 hours.
