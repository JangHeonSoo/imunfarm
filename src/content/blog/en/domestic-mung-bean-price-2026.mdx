Shop for hulled mung beans in Korea and the same 1 kg bag can cost KRW 5,000 or more than KRW 30,000. Most of the gap is origin: domestic beans cost two to three times as much as beans from Peru or China.

> Bottom line: <strong>On October 8, 2026, domestic mung beans (grade A, 40 kg) averaged KRW 551,400 across five wholesale cities, about KRW 13,785 per kilogram. Chinese beans (grade B) averaged KRW 269,200, about KRW 6,730 per kilogram.</strong> Online on October 11, most domestic hulled mung beans cost KRW 14,000–20,000 per kilogram, and hulled Peruvian beans about KRW 5,000.

## 2. Online prices

Checked on Coupang on October 11 (before coupons): domestic hulled mung beans at KRW 13,900 (1 kg), 16,900 (2 × 500 g), 19,900 (1 kg) and 9,900 (500 g, about 19,800 per kg); a cooperative-branded 500 g pack at KRW 16,980 (about 33,960 per kg); and hulled Peruvian beans at KRW 9,900 for 2 kg (about 4,950 per kg). Peru and China are the main suppliers; Peruvian mung beans have entered duty-free since 2020 under the Korea–Peru FTA.

## 3. Mung bean pancakes: how much and how long to soak

The Korean Food Promotion Institute recipe for four servings uses 500 g of dried mung beans, soaked in lukewarm water for about an hour, skinned and ground with ½ cup of water, then mixed with 250 g minced pork, 150 g mung bean sprouts and 250 g kimchi. Soaked, 500 g of beans becomes about 1 kg. The National Institute of Agricultural Sciences says whole beans with skins need about 8 hours of soaking before rubbing off the skins.

At current prices, 500 g of domestic hulled beans costs about KRW 7,000–10,000; Peruvian beans about KRW 2,500.

## 4. For growers: picking, drying and selling

Mung beans flower two or three times at 10–15 day intervals, so pods ripen unevenly. Nongsaro advises harvesting 40–50 days after flowering, by hand in three or four passes; pods caught in rain can sprout inside. A Jeju extension trial found two pickings 20 days apart gave 131 kg per 10a for 47.7 hours of labor, versus 136 kg and 51.6 hours for three pickings 15 days apart. In the south and Jeju, the Dahyeon variety sown July 20–30 can be combine-harvested in one pass in late October to early November. The RDA variety Chaeheun (bred 2024) resists pod shattering and suits machine harvest.

Weevils are the main storage threat, so dry thoroughly and store cold. The government purchase standard caps moisture at 14.0%, and the 2025-crop stockpile price was KRW 8,030 per kilogram for grade 1. Wholesale consignment fees for grains are capped at 2%.
