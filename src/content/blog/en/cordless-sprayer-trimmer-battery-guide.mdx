Engine trimmers mean mixed fuel, pull starts, noise and fumes, so many growers now look at cordless sprayers and trimmers. Buy them one at a time, though, and your shed fills with chargers and batteries from different brands. With cordless tools, choose the battery first.

> Bottom line: <strong>standardise on one battery platform.</strong> Same brand, same voltage lets your sprayer, trimmer and pruner share batteries and chargers. Choose a sprayer by the weight you can carry, and buy trimmer protection before the trimmer: Korea’s Consumer Agency logged 405 trimmer injuries in 2019–2023, 66% to feet and legs.

## 1. Why cordless.

No fuel mixing, push-button start, quiet, no exhaust: ideal for greenhouses and plots near home. The trade-off is runtime; large areas need several batteries.

## 2. Pick the battery first.

Batteries rarely work across brands. 18V and “20V MAX” are usually the same class; 36V/40V tools have more power for trimmers and large sprayers. Higher Ah lasts longer but weighs and costs more. Check the price of a spare battery and whether you can charge one while working. If you already own power tools, see whether that brand makes farm tools. Pruner buying criteria: [electric pruning shears](/en/blog/electric-pruning-shears-orchard-labor-saving/).

## 3. Sprayers: think weight.

A litre of water weighs 1 kg, so a full 20 L backpack tank is 20 kg plus the unit. Match tank size (16 L vs 20 L) to your daily area and strength, check how many tanks a battery covers, choose lance length for orchard or greenhouse, and make sure nozzles, hoses and pumps are easy to buy.

## 4. Trimmers: protection first.

Of 405 trimmer injuries (2019–2023), September had 143 and August 102; feet and legs took 66%, hands and arms 25%, head and face 5%, and about eight in ten were cuts. Line heads are safer on stony ground; metal blades handle tough growth but throw debris. Wear a face shield, safety glasses, knee pads, safety boots and gloves, keep people 15 m away, and remove the battery before clearing the head. Quiet tools make it easier to miss someone approaching.

## 5. Spraying pesticides.

Korea’s Rural Development Administration advises full-body protective clothing or waterproof rainwear, a tightly sealing mask, goggles, rubber gloves worn over the sleeves and taped, and tall boots; wash afterwards and launder protective clothing separately. A respirator with organic-vapour cartridges greatly reduces inhalation of spray; replace cartridges on schedule.
