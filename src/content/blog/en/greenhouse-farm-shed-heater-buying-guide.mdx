When the first frost warnings come in late October, greenhouse growers and farm-shed owners start shopping for heaters: electric, kerosene, gas, wood. Before price, decide three things: how much heat you need, which fuel you can use, and whether people breathe in that space.

> Bottom line: <strong>choose by heat output (kW), then fuel, then safety.</strong> 1 kW is about 860 kcal per hour. In greenhouses, sealing leaks is cheaper than a bigger heater: Korea’s Rural Development Administration found that closing gaps equal to just 0.6% of the curtain area cut heat loss by about 12%. If you burn fuel in a shed where people stay, a carbon monoxide alarm is essential.

## 1. Size first.

1 kW ≈ 860 kcal/h, so a 2 kW heater gives about 1,720 kcal/h. Need depends on the temperature gap and how fast heat leaks. For greenhouses, ask your local agricultural technology center for a heating-load assessment; for a small uninsulated shed used for short work sessions, people often start with a 1–2 kW electric heater.

## 2. Fuels compared.

| Fuel                    | Pros                            | Cons                                      | Suits                          |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Electric                | No combustion gas, easy install | Contracted power limits, electricity cost | Sheds, small greenhouse backup |
| Kerosene/diesel hot-air | High output                     | Needs exhaust, fuel handling              | Main greenhouse heating        |
| Gas (butane/LPG)        | Cheap, light                    | High CO risk; never in closed spaces      | Brief use in ventilated space  |
| Wood stove              | Cheap fuel                      | CO and fire risk, flue upkeep             | Spaces with a proper flue      |

Greenhouse kerosene/diesel heaters may qualify for tax-exempt farm fuel; ask your local cooperative.

## 3. Monthly electric cost.

2 kW × 8 hours × 30 days = 480 kWh. On KEPCO’s agricultural (B) low-voltage tariff (₩1,150/kW basic, ₩65.9/kWh, April 2025), that is about ₩34,000 a month before VAT and surcharges. Residential or general-service contracts cost much more, so check your contract type.

## 4. Seal leaks before upsizing.

RDA’s thermal-imaging diagnosis found that sealing gaps equal to 0.6% of the curtain area cut heat loss about 12%, and multi-layer thermal curtains can nearly halve heating costs. Check curtain seams, the bottom edge, doors, side vents and torn film. In sheds, draft strips and floor insulation often beat a bigger heater.

## 5. Carbon monoxide.

Korea’s fire agency recorded 471 CO poisoning incidents in 2019–2021; 85.1% happened October–March, with January highest (98). Causes split among gas (33.3%), coal (32.5%) and wood or charcoal (30.4%), with 27 cardiac arrests. CO is colourless and odourless. Install a CO alarm wherever fuel is burned, ventilate often, switch combustion heaters off before sleeping (farm sheds are not dwellings), and check flues every season.
