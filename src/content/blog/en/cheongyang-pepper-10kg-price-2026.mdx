Cheongyang peppers get expensive every winter. A 10 kg box bought in autumn, split between the fridge, the freezer and a jar of soy-sauce pickles, lasts into spring.

> Bottom line: <strong>On October 8, 2026, Cheongyang peppers (grade A, 10 kg) averaged KRW 47,960 across five wholesale cities, about KRW 4,800 per kilogram and 25% below a year earlier.</strong> Over 2016–2025, Seoul monthly averages were KRW 47,000–54,000 in October–December, then KRW 75,148 in January and KRW 98,642 in February. January beat October in nine of ten years.

## 3. Storing and pickling

Nongsaro notes that green peppers are sensitive to moisture and can blacken inside; keep unwashed portions in newspaper or a bag in the fridge, and wash and seed peppers for long storage. For the freezer, wash, dry thoroughly, cut and freeze flat in portions. The Nongsaro pepper pickle recipe (National Institute of Agricultural Sciences) uses 1 kg of green peppers with 300 g each of soy sauce, vinegar and sugar, no water: trim stems to 1 cm, nick the tips, pack the jar, pour in the boiled liquid hot and seal.

## 4. For growers

Peppers lose market value once frosted, so pick everything before the first frost. Late-season peppers often grade B, about KRW 20,000 less per 10 kg on October 8. Wholesale consignment fees for produce are capped at 7%. Selling the last pick directly as "pickling and freezing peppers" matches what buyers want; making and selling pickles yourself requires a food business report.
