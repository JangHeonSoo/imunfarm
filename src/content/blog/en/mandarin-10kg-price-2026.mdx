Mandarin season is here, and 10 kg boxes online range from under 20,000 won to over 30,000 won. Wholesale prices help narrow down what is normal.

> Bottom line: <strong>On November 16, 2026, KAMIS Seoul wholesale price for open-field mandarins (M size, 5 kg) was 13,700 won</strong>, or about 27,400 won for 10 kg. That is 9% below last November's average (15,000 won). In each of the last five winters, February was more expensive than November.

## Wholesale prices by city (November 16, 2026)

| City    |    M, 5 kg |    S, 5 kg |

| Seoul   | 13,700 won | 17,000 won |

| Gwangju | 22,300 won | 26,000 won |
| Daejeon | 12,600 won | 16,000 won |

Smaller fruit sells for more: S size was 24% above M in Seoul. Daily prices are on [mandarin (open-field) prices](/prices/mandarin-open/).

## Does waiting help?

Seoul monthly averages (M, 5 kg) from November to February rose in all five seasons from 2021–2022 to 2025–2026, by 12% to 103%. December was also higher than November every year. Jeju officials expect a larger 2026 crop (443,300 tonnes in the September survey, up 7.8% from 410,900 tonnes) with higher sugar content.

## Online boxes (November 17, Coupang Partners list, before account coupons)

A 5 kg "royal" (S/M) box was 16,050 won (3,210 won per kg), a 10 kg large-fruit box 18,900 won (1,890 won per kg) and 10 kg royal boxes 31,000 to 34,000 won. Compare by size and price per kg.

## For growers

Sorting matters most right now: S-size boxes earn about 24% more than M at wholesale. Holding fruit for later sale has paid off in past winters, but the gain ranged from about 10% to double, and storage needs 3–4°C and 85–90% humidity per Rural Development Administration guidance. Sell part now and store only what you can keep well.
