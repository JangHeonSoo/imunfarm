October evenings in the yard get cold as soon as the sun goes down. After a day at a weekend farm, or at dinner outside a rural home, a small wood stove makes the evening last longer. That is why searches for camping wood stoves climb to about 8,000 a month in Korea each autumn. The catch: prices run from KRW 20,000 to KRW 400,000, and a poor choice in something that burns can be dangerous.

> Bottom line: On October 13, 2026, camping wood stoves on Coupang cost KRW 20,000–40,000 for folding mini stoves, KRW 100,000–130,000 for small box stoves with flue sets, and KRW 160,000–260,000 for stoves with large glass windows. For yard use, check weight, flue height, glass and legs first. Do not use them inside tents, cars or farm sheds; keep a carbon monoxide alarm and extinguisher nearby. Lighting fires in or near forests is restricted by law.

Folding minis suit heating a pot or a short fire; their flues are short or absent, so smoke spreads at face height. For an hour or more of warmth in the yard, a box stove with a flue works better.

## 2. What to check

- **Weight and size:** light if you move it often; heavier is fine for a fixed spot.
- **Flue height:** smoke should exit above the head of a standing person.
- **Glass window:** heat-resistant glass lets you watch the fire with the door shut and reduces sparks.
- **Legs and base:** keeps heat off grass and decks.
- **Air control:** lets you turn the fire down and starve it when finishing.
- **Material:** thin sheet warps and rusts sooner; stainless, thick steel and cast iron trade durability for weight.

Do not rely on claims not on the product page. Coverage figures like "9 pyeong" do not hold in a windy yard.

## 3. Where to use it

This guide assumes open-air use: yards, decks, outside a campsite pitch. We do not recommend using wood stoves inside tents, cars or farm sheds, and we could not confirm official building and fire standards for fixed indoor installations, so ask an installer and your local fire station first.

Cheonan Seobuk Fire Station's winter camping advice: ventilate often when using heaters, always turn heaters off before sleeping, and install carbon monoxide detectors in enclosed spaces such as tents and cars. CO has no color, smell or taste; early symptoms are headache and nausea. If they appear, ventilate and call 119. Korea's fire agency counted 471 CO poisoning incidents from 2019 to 2021.

In the yard, clear dry grass, leaves, woodpiles, plastic and gas cylinders from around the stove; keep the flue away from eaves and branches; skip windy days; keep an extinguisher or water bucket within reach; and put ash in a lidded metal container only once embers are fully out.

Article 34 of Korea's Forest Protection Act prohibits lighting fires in forests and adjacent areas; local governments enforce it within 100 m of forests, with fines and possible criminal liability if a fire spreads. Check with your city or county forestry office before using a stove on land next to woods.

## 4. Firewood

Pohang Bukbu Fire Station advises using only well-dried wood, not stacking too much firewood, and cleaning soot from flues regularly. In Gyeongbuk, wood boiler fires from November 2024 to October 2025 rose 86% to 28, and 78.6% were caused by user carelessness. Wet wood smokes and coats flues with soot and tar faster. Dry pruned branches under cover first, cut wood to fit the door, never burn painted or treated wood, plywood or plastic, and do not overload a small stove.
