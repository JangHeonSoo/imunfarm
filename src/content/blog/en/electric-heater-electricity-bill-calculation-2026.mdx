When mornings turn cold, the electric heater comes out before the boiler. It is the easiest way to warm a small room, a bathroom or a corner of a farm hut. The bill is the problem: many households saw it double after running a heater for a few weeks last winter. The cost of an hour, or a month, can be worked out in advance.

> Bottom line: <strong>One hour of a 2 kW heater costs about KRW 302, 515 or 724 depending on your household's tier.</strong> A home using 250 kWh a month that runs a 2 kW heater four hours a day reaches 490 kWh, and the bill rises from about KRW 44,880 to KRW 122,540. One hour a day adds a little over KRW 15,000. Hours and tier matter more than the heater.

## 1. Start with kWh

**Power (W) × hours ÷ 1,000 = kWh.** A 2,000 W heater uses 2 kWh per hour, or 240 kWh over 30 days at four hours a day. Thermostat models cycle off at the set temperature, so actual use may be lower; a plug-in power meter measures it exactly.

## 2. KEPCO residential tiers (low voltage)

Rates have not changed since November 9, 2023, and remain frozen in Q4 2026.

| Monthly use   | Base charge | Energy charge per kWh |
| ------------- | ----------: | --------------------: |
| Up to 200 kWh |     KRW 910 |             KRW 120.0 |
| 201–400 kWh   |   KRW 1,600 |             KRW 214.6 |
| Over 400 kWh  |   KRW 7,300 |             KRW 307.3 |

Add the climate and environment charge (KRW 9.0/kWh) and the fuel cost adjustment (KRW 5.0/kWh for Q4 2026), then 10% VAT and the 2.7% electric power industry fund, rounding down to KRW 10.

| Tier          | Extra kWh (all-in, approx.) | 1 kW for 1 hour | 2 kW for 1 hour |
| ------------- | --------------------------: | --------------: | --------------: |
| Up to 200 kWh |                     KRW 151 |         KRW 151 |         KRW 302 |
| 201–400 kWh   |                     KRW 258 |         KRW 258 |         KRW 515 |
| Over 400 kWh  |                     KRW 362 |         KRW 362 |         KRW 724 |

In winter (December to February), use above 1,000 kWh is charged KRW 736.2/kWh.

## 3. Monthly increase for a 2 kW heater

| Usual monthly use (bill)   | 1 h/day (+60 kWh) | 2 h/day (+120 kWh) | 4 h/day (+240 kWh) |
| -------------------------- | ----------------: | -----------------: | -----------------: |
| 150 kWh (about KRW 23,670) |       +KRW 10,910 |        +KRW 26,370 |        +KRW 57,280 |
| 250 kWh (about KRW 44,880) |       +KRW 15,460 |        +KRW 30,920 |        +KRW 77,660 |
| 350 kWh (about KRW 70,650) |       +KRW 22,920 |        +KRW 44,650 |        +KRW 88,100 |

Crossing 400 kWh both raises the per-kWh rate and lifts the base charge from KRW 1,600 to KRW 7,300.

## 4. Cutting the cost

Use the lower power setting, set a timer, block drafts first, avoid sharing a power strip with other heaters, and compare with boiler fuel savings. Korea's National Fire Agency counted an average of 10,530 winter fires a year in 2019–2023, 24.42% from electrical causes.

## 5. Farm huts, storage sheds and camping

A farm hut on a residential contract uses the table above. On a general service (A) low-voltage contract there is no progressive pricing, but the base charge is KRW 6,160 per contracted kW and energy is KRW 119.0/kWh in winter and KRW 91.9/kWh in spring and autumn. The farm tariff (B, low voltage, KRW 65.9/kWh) is for uses such as seedling raising, crop cultivation, livestock, cold storage of produce and drying facilities; we found no basis for using it to heat living space in a farm hut. Campsites often cap wattage, and a 2 kW heater can exceed it.
