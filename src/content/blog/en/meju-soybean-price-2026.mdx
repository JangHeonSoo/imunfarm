When soybean leaves turn yellow in October, two questions come up at once. Households that make soybean paste ask what meju soybeans will cost this year. Growers ask whether to sell right after harvest or hold until meju-making season. Meju soybeans are white (yellow) soybeans, called baektae; KAMIS lists them as "white soybean (domestic)".

> Bottom line: <strong>In the KAMIS survey on October 12, 2026, domestic white soybeans (top grade, 40 kg) averaged KRW 228,600 at wholesale across five cities, about KRW 5,700 per kilogram</strong>, almost unchanged from a year earlier (−0.7%). Imported white soybeans averaged KRW 127,800 per 35 kg, about KRW 3,650 per kilogram. Domestic baektae sold online for KRW 6,750–7,950 per kilogram on October 13. Harvest when all leaves have dropped and 80–90% of pods are brown.

## 2. Online price of Korean meju soybeans

On October 13, domestic baektae on Coupang ranged from KRW 6,750 to 7,950 per kilogram (for example, 2 kg for KRW 13,500 and a 2025-crop 2 kg bag for KRW 15,900), while US-grown imported beans were about KRW 4,200 per kilogram. If a "domestic" bag is under KRW 3,000 per kilogram, check where the origin is actually stated. Most beans on sale now are 2025 crop; the 2026 crop arrives in volume from November once drying is done.

## 3. When and how meju is made

Traditionally, meju work begins in the eleventh lunar month, around December, once the weather turns cold. A government guide to Sunchang's traditional sauces describes the steps: soak 1 kg of beans in cold water for at least 10 hours (they roughly double in size), boil for at least two hours until they crush easily, mash and shape into blocks, dry in a ventilated place for 1–2 weeks, then ferment somewhere warm for 2–3 weeks. Korea's Ministry of Food and Drug Safety advises blocks of 1–2 kg so the inside dries properly, and a fermenting space below 30°C and 60% humidity.

Traditional units such as mal and doe are measures of volume, not weight, and markets convert them differently. Always ask for the weight in kilograms and compare the price per kilogram.

## 4. When to harvest soybeans

RDA puts the right time at when all leaves have fallen, stems have turned brown, and 80–90% of pods are brown. Gyeonggi Agricultural Research and Extension Services adds 7–14 days after pods reach their mature color. In RDA trials, harvesting late raised shattering losses and lowered germination:

| Harvest      | Shattering | Germination |
| ------------ | ---------: | ----------: |
| On time      |       0.1% |       95.8% |
| 20 days late |       5.0% |       91.3% |
| 40 days late |       9.4% |       87.5% |

Dry cut plants for 2–3 days to 13–15% grain moisture before threshing, run threshers at 300–400 rpm, dry in machines at 30°C or below, store at about 14% moisture, and keep cut plants out of the rain to prevent purple seed stain.

## 5. Sell now or hold until meju season?

With wholesale prices flat all year, holding for a price rise has weak support. What rises in late autumn is demand: searches for meju soybeans peak when households make meju. Growers with good drying and storage can pack 1–5 kg bags for direct sale then; others can sell part of the crop right away.
