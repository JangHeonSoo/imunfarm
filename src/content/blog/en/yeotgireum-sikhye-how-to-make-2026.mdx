Sikhye fails in two ways: the rice grains never float, or the drink turns sour. Both are temperature problems. Malt enzymes turn rice starch into sugar in warm water; too cool and it sours, too hot and the enzymes stop.

> Bottom line: <strong>cook 360g (2 cups) of rice firm, soak 115g (1 cup) malt powder in 2.4kg lukewarm water, strain, use only the clear top liquid, and keep at 60–65°C in a rice cooker for about 4 hours.</strong> It is done when 7–8 grains float. On November 9, 2026, Seoul wholesale rice (KAMIS) was KRW 62,000 per 20kg, so 2 cups of rice cost about 1,120.

## 1. Ratio

Korea.net's recipe series (Ministry of Culture, 2016): 115g malt powder, 2.4kg water at about 40°C, 360g rice cooked with 480g water, 160g sugar and a spoon of pine nuts. A 1999 study found the best saccharification at 4% malt, 20% rice, 60°C for 5 hours.

## 2. Saccharification

The Encyclopedia of Korean Culture says 60–70°C for 4–5 hours; Korea.net says 60–65°C for 3–4 hours. A 1997 study found malt enzymes work best at 60°C but weaken after an hour, and at 70°C less than 20% remains after 2 hours, so a longer hold does not add sweetness. Check at 3 hours the first time, since rice-cooker warm settings vary.

## 3. Boil, sweeten, store

Lift out and rinse the grains. Boil the liquid about 5 minutes, skimming foam, which also stops the enzymes. Sugar can go in while boiling (encyclopedia) or during the warm hold (Korea.net). No official shelf-life figure was found; refrigerate and drink soon, or freeze. Boiling the liquid down for a long time makes jocheong (grain syrup).

## 4. Making your own malt

From the National Institute of Crop Science (Nongsaro, 2017): use hulled barley less than a year old; soak in 16–20°C water in stages over 36–72 hours; spread 2–3cm deep on damp cloth in shade and keep turning and misting; by day 6 roots reach 1.5 times and shoots 2 times the grain length; dry quickly starting at 35°C, then 45°C and 55°C to about 10% moisture; remove roots, then grind and sieve. Best at 10–15°C average temperature. A 2016 study of 24 Korean barley varieties found peak saccharifying power on day 4.

## 5. For farmers

Recommended malting varieties include Hyemi, Hyeyang, Hyedang, Dahye and Hyeda. An RDA process (2023 report) cuts malting to 6.3 days. Demand for malt rises before Lunar New Year; check with the local sanitation office before selling malt powder.
