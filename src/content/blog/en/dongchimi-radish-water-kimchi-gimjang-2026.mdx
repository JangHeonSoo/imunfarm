After the cabbage kimchi is stuffed, a few radishes are always left over. In many Korean homes they go into a jar of dongchimi, the clear radish water kimchi served cold through winter. It takes fewer ingredients than cabbage kimchi, but how salty the brine is decides how the broth tastes in January.

> Bottom line: <strong>In the KAMIS survey on October 29, 2026, a 20kg box of autumn radish (top grade) sold at wholesale in Seoul for KRW 12,300, about KRW 620 per kg.</strong> Highland radish was KRW 16,600 (about KRW 830/kg). The Pusan National University Kimchi Research Institute recipe salts 10 small radishes overnight with 2 cups of coarse salt, then covers them with brine made from 1 cup of salt in 20 cups of water. Research shows dongchimi ferments faster at higher temperatures and higher salt levels.

## 2. The shopping list

The Pusan National University recipe uses 10 dongchimi radishes, 2 cups of coarse salt, 20 pickled green chilies, 5 red chilies, half a bunch each of small green onions and mustard greens, 1 pear, 1 head of garlic, 1 knob of ginger, and brine of 20 cups water with 1 cup salt.

Radish size varies, so weigh them. If 10 radishes weigh 10kg, the Seoul wholesale cost is about KRW 6,200. Pear (Shingo, 15kg) was KRW 57,200 and domestic ginger (10kg) KRW 61,600 in Seoul on the same day; dongchimi needs only one pear and a knob of ginger.

## 3. Choosing dongchimi radish

"Dongchimi radish" is a market name, not a variety. It means a small, firm radish that fits whole in the jar. Pick radishes that feel heavy for their size, do not give when pressed, and have smooth skin, since they go in unpeeled. The greens can be dried into [siraegi](/en/blog/dried-radish-greens-siraegi-2026/).

## 4. Method and brine ratio

1. Remove the fine roots, wash, coat the radishes with coarse salt and leave them overnight in the jar.
2. Pickle green chilies in brine for about a week (or buy them pickled).
3. Lightly salt the green onions and mustard greens and tie them in small bundles. Halve the pear with the skin on.
4. Slice garlic and ginger into a cloth pouch.
5. Dissolve 1 cup salt in 20 cups water and strain.
6. Put the pouch in the jar, layer the radishes and other ingredients, weigh them down with a stone and pour in the brine.

## 5. When it is ready

A 1996 study (Chang et al., Korean Society for Applied Biological Chemistry) fermented dongchimi in 2.0%, 3.2% and 5.1% brine at 4, 10 and 20°C. Fermentation was faster at higher temperatures and higher salt levels, and radishes softened more at higher temperatures. A 1997 study on low-salt dongchimi juice found 0.5% brine at 20°C scored best in taste tests, but that was a quick-drinking juice, not a winter batch. At home: do not leave the jar warm for long, eat low-salt batches first, and keep the radishes submerged.

## 6. For growers

Small, even radishes sell as "dongchimi radish" alongside kimjang shopping in November. Harvest or cover before forecast frost. Radish keeps best near 0°C at 90–95% humidity; see the [cold storage temperature table](/en/blog/cold-storage-temperature-humidity-by-crop/). Wholesale prices are what intermediaries charge, so farm-gate prices are lower.
