A night in an off-grid farm shed needs a light, phone charging and, in winter, an electric blanket. For a shed visited once or twice a weekend, a fixed solar system is hard to justify, so many people look at portable power stations. The key is to calculate before buying.

> Bottom line: <strong>Estimate runtime as capacity (Wh) × 0.85 ÷ load (W).</strong> A 300 Wh unit covers lights, phones and a laptop for a night; running a 150 W electric blanket at full power for eight hours takes about 1,400 Wh. LFP batteries last 3,000–4,000 cycles but must not be charged below 0°C. Since April 20, 2026, Korea allows at most two power banks of 160 Wh or less on board, so most camping power stations cannot fly. On October 11, Coupang listed the Jackery 300 Plus (288 Wh) at KRW 359,000 and the Explorer 1000 v2 (1,070 Wh) at KRW 890,000.

## 1. Wh and W

Capacity (Wh) is how much energy the battery holds. Rated output (W) is how much power it can deliver at once. A large battery with a 300 W inverter still cannot run a kettle or heater.

## 2. Runtime

Converting DC to AC loses energy, so Jackery's runtime examples use 85% of capacity. Jackery Korea says the 300 Plus runs a 55 W electric blanket for about 4 hours; 288 × 0.85 ÷ 55 is about 4.4 hours.

| Load                     |      245 Wh |      288 Wh |     1,024 Wh |     1,070 Wh |
| ------------------------ | ----------: | ----------: | -----------: | -----------: |
| CCTV camera (5 W)        |  about 42 h |  about 49 h |  about 174 h |  about 182 h |
| LED light (10 W)         |  about 21 h |  about 24 h |   about 87 h |   about 91 h |
| Laptop charging (60 W)   | about 3.5 h | about 4.1 h | about 14.5 h | about 15.2 h |
| Electric blanket (150 W) | about 1.4 h | about 1.6 h |  about 5.8 h |  about 6.1 h |

Blankets cycle on and off once warm, so real use is lower than the label. A 1 kW heater would drain a 1,070 Wh unit in about 54 minutes.

## 4. Winter, solar and storage

Do not charge below 0°C (Jackery says it causes irreversible damage) and do not use below −10°C. A 100 W panel makes roughly 0.35–0.4 kWh a day, so refilling a 1,070 Wh unit takes about three days, longer in winter. Check each unit's solar input limit before buying a panel. For long storage, Jackery advises about 50% charge, recharging every three months, at 0–40°C. Do not leave batteries in hot cars.

For a shed used daily with a fridge and CCTV, a fixed solar system is the better fit. Portable units suit weekend visits, car camping and power cuts.

## 5. Flights and trains

From April 20, 2026, Korea's transport ministry allows two power banks of 160 Wh or less per passenger and bans charging them or using them to charge devices on board. Korean Air says batteries over 160 Wh can be neither carried on nor checked. Since July 1, 2026, lithium batteries over 160 Wh are also reported to be banned on Korean trains; check Korail's notice.
